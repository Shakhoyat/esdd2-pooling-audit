\section{Theorem 1: statement and full proof}

\vspace{2pt}\noindent\textbf{Theorem 1 (collapsibility): } \emph{Let $T$ be a ROC summary,
continuous, non-decreasing under pointwise dominance and strictly so under strict dominance, on a convex class $\mathcal{R}$
whose image $T[\mathcal{R}]$ is an interval and in which every $R$ is strictly dominated by, and strictly dominates, curves attaining every value of $T[\mathcal{R}]$ above and below $T(R)$. Then $T$ admits an aggregation rule $T_{\mathrm{pub}} = \varphi(T_0,
T_{\mathrm{a}}, w_0)$ for all curves and all $w_0$ if and only if $T = g \circ L$ with $g$
continuous and strictly monotone and $L[\mathrm{ROC}] = c + \int_0^1 \mathrm{ROC}\,dW$ for a fixed
finite non-negative Borel measure $W$, in which case $\varphi$ is the quasi-arithmetic mean generated by $g^{-1}$.}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Sufficiency.} With $T=g\circ L$ and $w_0+w_{\mathrm{a}}=1$ the constant is absorbed, so $L$ is
affine under mixing; writing $L=g^{-1}\!\circ T$ and applying $g$ gives the rule with
$\varphi(x,y,w)=g\big(w\,g^{-1}(x)+(1-w)g^{-1}(y)\big)$.

\emph{Necessity.} Fix $w$ and set $x\oplus y=\varphi(x,y,w)$ on $I=T[\mathcal{R}]$. Mixing is
idempotent, so $x\oplus x=x$; $\oplus$ is strictly increasing in each argument because mixing
preserves pointwise dominance; and it is bisymmetric, since $(A\oplus B)\oplus(C\oplus D)$ and
$(A\oplus C)\oplus(B\oplus D)$ are both $w^2A+w(1-w)(B+C)+(1-w)^2D$. Continuity is inherited from
$T$, and the richness hypothesis carries all four to $I^4$. Acz\'el~\cite[\S6.4]{aczel1966} then gives a
continuous strictly monotone $k_w$ and $\alpha(w)\in(0,1)$ with $x\oplus y=k_w^{-1}\big(\alpha(w)
k_w(x)+(1-\alpha(w))k_w(y)\big)$. Mixing at $v$ after $w$ is mixing at weights $vw,\,v(1-w),\,1-v$,
so $\{\oplus_w\}$ is mutually bisymmetric, which for quasi-arithmetic means forces a common generator $k$~\cite[Chs.~17--18]{aczel1989}; idempotence and the same composition then make $\alpha$ multiplicative and
continuous, so $\alpha(w)=w$. Hence $L:=k\circ T$ is affine on $\mathcal{R}$ and continuous in the
sup norm, so it extends to a continuous affine functional on the closed convex hull of $\mathcal{R}$
in $C[0,1]$; by the Riesz representation theorem every such functional is $c+\int_0^1\mathrm{ROC}\,dW$
for a finite signed Borel $W$, and monotonicity of $T$ forces $W\ge 0$. Take $g:=k^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

\section{Lemma and Proposition 2}

\vspace{2pt}\noindent\textbf{Lemma: } \emph{If $T$ admits an aggregation rule $\varphi$ and
$T(R_0)=T(R_0')$, then $T(wR_0+(1-w)S)=T(wR_0'+(1-w)S)$ for every $S\in\mathcal{R}$ and every $w$,
because both sides equal $\varphi(T(R_0),T(S),w)$.}

\vspace{2pt}\noindent\textbf{Proposition 2: } \emph{The equal error rate, the minimum detection
cost and Youden's index admit no aggregation rule.}

\begin{proof}
Let $A$, $B$ and $S$ join $(0,0)$ to $(1,1)$ by straight segments through the interior breakpoints
$(\tfrac25,\tfrac35)$; $(\tfrac3{11},\tfrac12),(\tfrac25,\tfrac35)$; and $(\tfrac27,1)$. All
three are concave, hence equal to their own convex hulls. Curves $A$ and $B$ share an equal error
rate of $\tfrac25$ yet differ as curves ($\mathrm{AUC}$ $\tfrac35$ against $\tfrac{34}{55}$), and
mixing each with the \emph{same} $S$ at $w=\tfrac12$ gives $\tfrac27$ and $\tfrac3{11}$; both
pooled crossings fall on curve vertices, so they are exact rather than interpolated. A second
triple, through $(\tfrac13,\tfrac23)$; $(\tfrac16,\tfrac12),(\tfrac13,\tfrac23)$; and
$(\tfrac15,\tfrac45)$, shares a minimum detection cost and an index of $\tfrac13$ at equal priors
and unit costs, and pools to $\tfrac7{24}$ against $\tfrac4{15}$ and to $\tfrac5{12}$ against
$\tfrac7{15}$. Each pair contradicts the Lemma, so no $\varphi$ exists; on the same curves every
weighted area has residual exactly zero. A third triple, in the released code, separates the minimum detection cost at the ASVspoof 5 cost parameters.
\end{proof}

\section{Theorem 3: statement and full argument}

\vspace{2pt}\noindent\textbf{Theorem 3 (sharp partial identification): } \emph{A published table
fixing $w_0$ and $\mathrm{EER}_{\text{pub}}$ confines the component-only value to}
\begin{equation}
\Big[\max\Big(0,\tfrac{\mathrm{EER}_{\text{pub}} - w_0}{w_{\mathrm{a}}}\Big),\;
      \min\Big(1,\tfrac{\mathrm{EER}_{\text{pub}}}{w_{\mathrm{a}}}\Big)\Big],
\label{eq:interval}
\end{equation}
\emph{and both endpoints are attained, so the interval is the sharp identified set given $(w_0,\mathrm{EER}_{\text{pub}})$.}

\noindent Write $\tau^\ast$ for the threshold at which the published rates cross, so
$\mathrm{FAR}(\tau^\ast) = \mathrm{FRR}_{\text{pub}}(\tau^\ast) = \mathrm{EER}_{\text{pub}}$. Evaluating
(1) there, with $\mathrm{FRR}_0(\tau^\ast) \in [0,1]$ the only freedom, gives
$\mathrm{FRR}_{\mathrm{a}}(\tau^\ast) \in [(\mathrm{EER}_{\text{pub}} - w_0)/w_{\mathrm{a}},\,
\mathrm{EER}_{\text{pub}}/w_{\mathrm{a}}]$. Because $\mathcal{N}$ is shared the false-acceptance
rate is common to both components, so $\mathrm{FAR}_{\mathrm{a}}(\tau^\ast) =
\mathrm{EER}_{\text{pub}}$; since $\mathrm{FAR}$ falls and $\mathrm{FRR}_{\mathrm{a}}$ rises in $\tau$, the component-only crossing $\mathrm{EER}_{\mathrm{a}}$ lies between
$\mathrm{FAR}_{\mathrm{a}}(\tau^\ast)$ and $\mathrm{FRR}_{\mathrm{a}}(\tau^\ast)$. As
$(\mathrm{EER}_{\text{pub}} - w_0)/w_{\mathrm{a}} \le \mathrm{EER}_{\text{pub}} \le
\mathrm{EER}_{\text{pub}}/w_{\mathrm{a}}$ whenever $\mathrm{EER}_{\text{pub}} \le 1$, that bracket
is contained in (3); intersecting with $[0,1]$ gives the stated form. Both
directions are checked numerically in the released code.

Both endpoints are attained, so (3) is sharp given $(w_0,\mathrm{EER}_{\text{pub}})$:
for every sampled value in it, endpoints included, the released code builds an admissible system
matching both crossings to $10^{-12}$. Conditioning additionally on the other published cells of a
particular submission constrains the construction further; there we exhibit a witness for the lower
endpoint only, so under that stronger conditioning (3) is an outer bound. Bounding a component functional
under a two-component mixture is classical~\cite{horowitz1995}; (3) bounds from a
single published scalar. The reference baseline's environmental row on the evaluation split
has $w_0=0.5323$ and $\mathrm{EER}_{\text{pub}}=0.4336$, so (3) returns
$[0,\,0.9271]$, that is $93\%$ of the range, containing the true $0.2090$ along with almost
everything else.

\section{Passages shortened in the conference version}

\subsection*{Applicability signal (paper \S3.1)}

The official submission format requires a predicted class and three scores, including a
score for the \texttt{original} class~\cite{esdd2plan}. While this is not an explicit
applicability flag, it is a strong signal: on the reference system, this
score separates the structural zeros with an AUC of $0.9964$.

\subsection*{Acoustic difficulty versus pooling (paper \S4.3)}

A natural objection is that environmental spoofing is simply harder to detect than speech manipulation, so the higher pooled error reflects the model rather than the protocol. A controlled ablation separates the two.

When evaluated strictly on mixed classes where genuine component annotations exist, the environmental and speech heads achieve comparable EERs of $0.2090$ and $0.1906$ respectively, a marginal intrinsic difference of only $0.0184$.

When structural zeros are reintroduced under the benchmark mapping, the environmental EER rises by $0.2246$, whereas the speech EER shifts by merely $0.0087$. This allows an exact additive decomposition of the reported $0.2343$ performance gap:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\mathrm{total}} = \Delta_{\mathrm{pooling}} + \Delta_{\mathrm{intrinsic}} = 0.2159 + 0.0184 = 0.2343,
\end{equation}
where the net contribution from the pooling convention alone ($0.2246 - 0.0087 = 0.2159$) accounts for over $92\%$ of the observed disparity.

\subsection*{Applicability-conditioned evaluation (paper \S4.4)}

Table~2 outlines what an external reader can recover from outside the scoring
process. We set $\gamma_a = 1-\sigma(s_{\mathrm{o}})$, with $s_{\mathrm{o}}$ the submitted
\texttt{original} score and $\sigma$ the logistic function, and score each axis over $\{x:\gamma_a(x)\ge\tfrac12\}$, at a precision of $0.999$ and a recall of
$0.943$. The absolute deviation from the component-only value falls by $97\%$ on the environmental
axis and by $78\%$ on the speech axis, and sweeping the gate threshold from $0.1$ to $0.9$ moves the
environmental error only between $0.0036$ and $0.0096$.

\subsection*{Relation to prior work (paper \S2)}

\noindent\textbf{Relation to prior work: } The integral class is not new~\cite{wieand1989}, and
characterizations of the same shape exist for confusion counts~\cite{prati2026}, association
measures~\cite{kenah2025}, and fairness auditing under unobserved protected
class~\cite{kallus2019}. Those aggregate over groups that carry their own negatives, and in that
configuration even the area stops aggregating: it acquires cross-group terms and can fall outside the
range of the group values~\cite{matos2026}. Equation~(2) instead splits the positive side
against a shared negative set. The cross terms vanish, the area returns to the collapsible side, and
what is left outside the class is the fixed point. It also separates the setting from selective prediction~\cite{elyaniv2010}, where the model
abstains rather than the protocol pooling inapplicable inputs. Within anti-spoofing, unease about
the equal error rate is not new: the t-DCF was introduced because the EER operating point does not
reflect application requirements~\cite{kinnunen2020tdcf}, and the t-EER removes its free parameters
in a tandem setting~\cite{kinnunen2024teer}. Those lines change the operating point or the cost
model while holding the evaluation population fixed. Here the population itself carries rows to
which the axis does not apply.
